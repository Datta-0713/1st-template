About this program

Deep Ocean Mapping is the foundational ABYSS field program — a systematic, multi-deployment survey of Pacific seamount and abyssal province environments from the coastal shelf edge to 6,000m depth. The program's central objective is to create the first longitudinal baseline record for these environments at the resolution required to detect meaningful change.

Across eight deployments since 2024, the program has produced the most complete spatial record of its target province ever assembled, including the first centimeter-scale bathymetric models of three previously unmapped seamount systems and an expanding time series of water column conditions at 14 fixed sampling stations.

The program operates on a continuous re-survey cycle, with field teams returning to the same coordinates every six months to update the baseline and detect statistically significant changes in physical, chemical, and biological conditions.

Methodology

How we survey at scale

The survey approach combines satellite data with in-situ AUV operations and repeat sampling at fixed stations. Each deployment follows a standardized protocol to ensure comparability between visits — allowing change detection without confounding from methodological variation.

01

Site Selection

Satellite altimetry, historical expedition data, and predictive terrain models identify candidate provinces and define priority depth transects before any vehicle enters the water.

02

Systematic Survey

AUV formations conduct overlapping survey passes across the target province, with redundant coverage at key depth transitions. All track lines, sensor logs, and navigation data are archived with full provenance.

03

Temporal Re-survey

Return missions every six months repeat the same survey protocol at the same coordinates. Difference models between visit epochs form the change detection record that gives the program its scientific value.

What we found

Key findings to date

Finding 01

Unmapped terrain

34% of the surveyed area revealed previously uncharted seamount structures at resolutions insufficient in prior global datasets. Three distinct summit systems were mapped for the first time, with peak reliefs of 800–1,400m above the surrounding abyssal plain.

Finding 02

Signal convergence zones

Three zones of anomalous acoustic and thermal overlap were identified at depth, corresponding with localized biological aggregations in optical survey data. The co-occurrence suggests previously undescribed mid-water habitat features.

Finding 03

Habitat change

Measurable changes in megafaunal distribution were documented between the first and second complete survey cycle (six months apart). The changes are spatially correlated with temperature anomalies at 3,200m depth, providing the first direct linkage of benthic community shifts to a measured physical forcing.

Finding 04

Temporal sensitivity

Environmental conditions at 3,200m show a 4-month lag to surface forcing events, consistent with model predictions but now quantified directly from in-situ data for this province for the first time. The lag structure varies with depth — shallower features respond faster than deeper ones.
