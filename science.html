ABYSS — Science

Science Built for the Ocean's Complexity.

We design observation systems around the way the ocean actually works — not around what is convenient to measure.

Our approach

Longitudinal observation over point sampling.

The ocean is not static between observations. Treating it as such — building a picture from isolated measurements taken years apart — produces a record that is not a picture of the ocean, but a picture of our ability to reach it.

The ABYSS approach is built around temporal density rather than spatial coverage alone. Repeated observation of the same province, across the same depth intervals, at intervals short enough to resolve seasonal and event-scale change — that is how you build a field intelligence model rather than a data archive.

Each ABYSS field program is designed around a multi-year observation commitment. We do not do one-time surveys. We return to the same coordinates until the change record is meaningful.

Mesopelagic

ABYSS tracks the deep scattering layer, carbon flux from sinking particles, acoustic backscatter profiles, dissolved oxygen minimum zones, and diel vertical migrators. This is the ocean's primary carbon pump — and one of the least-sampled regions on Earth.

Bathypelagic

No sunlight reaches here. ABYSS uses acoustic imaging, chemical sensors for hydrogen sulfide and methane plumes, and passive acoustic monitoring for biological activity. Deep currents and thermohaline circulation dynamics are tracked through temperature and salinity profiling.

What we monitor

Six signal types. One coherent field picture.

Physical

Temperature · Salinity · Pressure

The fundamental hydrographic backbone of every field program. Profiled at continuous depth resolution.

Chemical

O₂ · CO₂ · Nutrients · Trace metals

Dissolved gas concentrations and nutrient profiles that map productivity, carbon cycling, and ecosystem state.

Acoustic

Active sonar · Passive ADCP · Bioacoustics

Backscatter imagery, current profiling, and passive recording of biological and geological sound sources.

Optical

Fluorescence · Turbidity · Camera imagery

Light-based measurements from fluorometers and optical backscatter sensors, plus high-resolution video surveys.

Biological

eDNA · Macrofauna · Microbial

Environmental DNA sampling, visual biological surveys, and proxy indicators of microbial biomass and activity.

Temporal

Change rates · Lag structure · Trends

The time dimension: how conditions change between visits, and what the structure of that change tells us about forcing mechanisms.
