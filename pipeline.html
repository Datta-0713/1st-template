ABYSS — Pipeline

From Signal to Understanding.

A four-stage pipeline that turns ocean observations into a continuously evolving field intelligence model.

Four stages
MAP SENSE MODEL UNDERSTAND

The ABYSS pipeline is not a linear process that terminates when data is collected. Each stage feeds back into the one before it. Modelling results inform where and when to sense. Sensing reveals gaps in the spatial map. Understanding — what we can finally say about the ocean state — generates the next generation of observing questions.

This is a continuous cycle. The pipeline operates indefinitely, as long as the field program runs. It does not produce a final report. It produces a continuously updated representation of a living ocean system.

Stage 01

Map

Before sensing begins, we build the spatial context. The MAP stage uses existing bathymetric datasets, satellite altimetry, prior expedition records, and model predictions to construct the working spatial model of the target province. This is not just a map — it is a set of spatial hypotheses about where interesting signals are most likely to occur.

ABYSS refines this map continuously. Every new survey pass updates the spatial model with higher-resolution data, correcting errors in prior datasets and revealing features that were invisible at lower resolution. Unmapped seamounts, canyon systems, and sediment transport paths emerge through accumulated observation.

The spatial model drives AUV mission planning. Path optimization algorithms use the map to maximize coverage efficiency while ensuring that statistically important regions receive proportionally more observation time.

Stage 02

Sense

Autonomous systems enter the field. The SENSE stage is where raw environmental signals are acquired: temperature, salinity, pressure, dissolved gases, acoustic backscatter, optical properties, biological indicators, and chemical tracers. Every measurement is time-stamped and geo-referenced with sub-meter accuracy.

ABYSS instruments are designed around modularity. A mission profile is selected based on the scientific questions being addressed. The sensor stack changes between deployments. What stays constant is the quality control framework — calibration standards, redundancy requirements, and data provenance recording.

Field teams maintain real-time communication with AUVs via acoustic modems. Mission parameters can be updated mid-deployment as the emerging data reveals unexpected conditions in the field.

Stage 03

Model

Raw observations are calibrated, quality-controlled, and integrated into the ABYSS field model. The MODEL stage is where measurements become understanding: spatial interpolation fills gaps between survey tracks, temporal models reconstruct what was happening between visits, and multi-parameter correlations surface non-obvious relationships.

The field model is not a single fixed representation. It is a probabilistic estimate of the ocean state, updated with every new observation. Uncertainty is treated as first-class information — every model output includes confidence bounds, which narrow as the observation record grows.

Machine learning models are trained on the accumulating field record, identifying patterns in the data that inform future sensing strategies. The model learns the ocean's temporal rhythms and uses them to anticipate conditions between deployments.

Stage 04

Understand

The UNDERSTAND stage is the interface between the ABYSS field model and the humans who need to act on it. Field teams receive structured summaries of what changed since the last visit. Researchers receive queryable data with documented provenance. Partners receive atlas tiles and change maps for their specific region of interest.

Understanding is not the same as explaining. The ABYSS platform does not draw conclusions about why the ocean changed. It documents what changed, with what confidence, and at what resolution. Interpretation is the work of the researchers who built the scientific questions the pipeline was designed to address.

The UNDERSTAND output feeds directly back into the MAP stage. Where the field model is confident, future surveys can be optimized for efficiency. Where it is uncertain, the next deployment is directed to fill the gaps.

Data standards

Three non-negotiable data principles

01

Spatial integrity

Every observation is geo-referenced with full provenance: positioning system, accuracy estimate, and vessel/vehicle attitude at the time of measurement. Positional uncertainty is never removed — it is preserved alongside the data.

02

Temporal continuity

Gaps in the time series are modelled rather than filled with assumptions. The model makes explicit what it is inferring versus what was directly observed, and the confidence bound reflects the gap duration and data density on either side.

03

Chain of custody

From sensor calibration through to final field atlas tile, every transformation applied to the data is documented and reversible. No black-box processing steps. Every output can be traced back to specific field measurements.
